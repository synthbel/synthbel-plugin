# Participation scenarios alongside disclosure judgments

Use this reference when the request includes refusals, interrupted interviews or missing participants. Keep the completed-audience disclosure result intact and identify the new audience that an inclusion scenario would represent.

Recover invitations, starts, eligibility/screening, quota exits, interruptions and completions separately. Preserve each denominator and distinguish raw counts from survey-weight mass. A printed dropoff rate with no definition is a proxy candidate, not a verified response rate. An unknown rate stays unknown. A field called `Status` may identify a panel rather than completion; selected partial records may cover only a small share of all interruptions.

Let `p_star` denote the corrected distribution among completers and `q_star` a proposed already fear-free distribution among missing participants. Under common-target weights, the combined distribution is `(1-r_eff)p_star + r_eff q_star`, and participation adds `r_eff(q_star-p_star)`. Choose and explain a central `q_star` plus meaningful alternatives, preserving every original answer category. Always show the same-answer comparator `q_star = p_star`, whose added participation effect is zero; it is a comparison, not a mandatory central choice or evidence that selection is absent. Greater uncertainty, less criticism and more criticism can each be appropriate. Label the direction of a chosen tilt as unsupported when no source establishes it; the interruption rate alone supplies none of these opinions.

State the assumptions behind `r_eff`, which is missing weight divided by total weight on the same intended target. If it is approximated by a raw rate, label every combined number conditional on that proxy. Vary rate and missing opinions separately. Without a usable rate, give a formula or a clearly hypothetical increment rather than borrowing another survey's rate. No extra disclosure correction is applied to `q_star`.

Keep composition-only diagnostics separate. Transporting dropout demographic margins from another wave requires transportability assumptions, and reconstructing joint margins requires a stated method. Combining those raw differences with an already demographically calibrated estimate can double count adjustment. Within-demographic-cell selection remains possible even when quotas match.

Deliver the original answers, corrected completer answers, assumed missing-group answers and conditional combined answers as distinct quantities. Label combined chart rows with both the assumed missing-group answers and any rate proxy. Report whether adding missing respondents changes the substantive conclusion and which assumption causes the change. These scenarios do not recover noncontacts or population opinion from a nonprobability sample by themselves.
